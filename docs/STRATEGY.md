# DocDoor — Strategic Evaluation

**On-Demand Home-Visit Doctor Platform (Turkey) — Strategy & 5-Year Roadmap**
_March 2026_

> Translated and adapted from the original strategy report. **Detailed financial projections (cash-flow, revenue, valuation and round sizes) are intentionally omitted** from this public version; only the strategic reasoning is included.

---

## 1. Business Model

DocDoor is a B2C on-demand home-visit doctor marketplace. A patient shares their location in the mobile app, the platform matches the nearest available doctor, and the doctor visits the patient's home for the consultation. Revenue comes from a **per-session commission (~20%)**, doctor promotion/visibility, and (later) subscription packages.

**Market context.** Turkey has ~85M people and 500M+ annual outpatient visits; average outpatient waits run 2–4 hours. The home-health segment is large and almost entirely undigitized — demand exists and need not be created; the challenge is *serving* it.

**Investment thesis.** Access and queue problems in the Turkish health system are structural and growing; home consultation shifts from luxury to necessity, and the first platform to scale captures network effects. This holds **only if** (a) the regulatory barrier is cleared, (b) the first cohort of doctors is secured, and (c) unit economics turn positive.

## 2. Five-Phase Roadmap

| Phase | Window | Focus |
|-------|--------|-------|
| 1 · Survival | Month 1–6 | MVP, first doctors, product-market-fit test |
| 2 · Early traction | Month 7–12 | Growth acceleration, ops team, Series-A prep |
| 3 · Growth | Month 13–24 | Multi-city (Ankara, İzmir), telehealth, insurance pilot |
| 4 · Maturity | Month 25–36 | Capacity solution (full-time + telehealth), path to profitability |
| 5 · Scale | Month 37–60 | Sustainable growth, B2B data platform, regional/exit options |

**Key milestones:** PMF test at month 6 (retention/NPS gates); Series-A as the "fate month" around the growth phase; telehealth launched before the capacity wall (~month 30); break-even only in the long run after disciplined cost control.

---

## 3. Doctor Acquisition — the #1 Risk

The platform only works with enough doctors: no doctors → patients wait → trust breaks → platform dies. Winning the first ~30 doctors is a survival issue.

**Channels (by phase):** personal network (highest conversion, earliest) → medical-school relationships → LinkedIn/doctor forums → doctor referrals → hospital partnerships → full-time hiring (from ~month 24).

**Retention (doctor churn assumed ~4%/month):**
- Guaranteed minimum income for the first months (paid even with no patients)
- Flexible scheduling (evenings / weekends only)
- Platform-paid malpractice insurance (zero risk to the doctor)
- Fast payouts (within 24h of a completed session)
- Capacity management — route demand to under-utilized doctors

---

## 4. User Growth

**Channels:** organic/SEO (slow, sustainable) · social media (target: parents, 25–45) · Google Ads (high intent, expensive CPC) · referral program (target viral coefficient ~0.3) · pharmacy partnerships (trust transfer) · corporate B2B (from ~month 12).

**Conversion funnel:** download 100% → registration ~60% (phone + address barrier) → first session ~15% (healthcare trust barrier) → second session ~35% (depends on first experience) → monthly active 4–10% (people aren't sick every month) → referral ~8%.

## 5. Unit Economics (qualitative)

The model targets a **healthy LTV/CAC** (broadly in the ~2.4–3.3× range early, with >3× as the goal at scale). Per-session margin after support, payment processing and the insurance share is positive. The two structural pressures are **high CAC** (trust barrier in healthcare) and **low usage frequency** — both addressed by referrals, pharmacy partnerships, insurance integration and chronic-care subscriptions. _(Concrete figures omitted in this public version.)_

## 6. Top 7 Risks & Stress Tests

1. **Regulatory barrier** — likelihood high, impact existential. A ministry ban or heavy licensing could halt operations. *Mitigation: health-law counsel from day 1, proactive ministry dialogue, parallel telehealth license, appointment-platform pivot.*
2. **Doctor supply crisis** — very likely, critical. Failing to win/retain the first cohort caps capacity. *Mitigation: guaranteed income, personal network, referral bonuses, platform-paid insurance.*
3. **Running out of cash** — high, existential. Seed runway is tight before the growth round. *Mitigation: start the raise early, keep a bridge ready, founder-salary cut plan, 3-month reserve rule.*
4. **Big-player entry** — medium, high. Getir/Hepsiburada/Doctolib with deep budgets. *Mitigation: speed (build the network first), exclusive doctor contracts, acqui-hire exit option.*
5. **Malpractice incident** — low likelihood, existential impact. *Mitigation: 3-stage verification (diploma, registry, background), mandatory insurance, crisis-comms plan.*
6. **Capacity bottleneck** — near-certain, high. Part-time capacity saturates. *Mitigation: telehealth, full-time pilot, nurse category, smart routing.*
7. **Low retention** — high, high. *Mitigation: chronic-care program, family subscription, seasonal campaigns, 48h follow-up, NPS >50.*

---

## 7. Breaking Points

- **Month 1–3 — first doctor crisis:** without enough doctors and good early sessions, pivot.
- **Month 6 — PMF test:** retention/NPS/growth gates, or pivot/close.
- **Month 10–12 — "valley of death":** seed runway runs low; the growth round must materialize.
- **Growth round — "fate month":** closes → full gas; doesn't → shrink and bridge.
- **~Month 30 — capacity wall:** telehealth must already be live.
- **Long-run break-even:** if not reached on plan, the model is structurally broken.

---

## 8. Investment-Committee Verdict (qualitative)

**Decision: conditional YES — only if specific milestones are met.**

**Supporting:** no direct competitor + real first-mover advantage; large addressable market; healthy-but-improvable unit economics; marketplace network effects as a moat; structural demand from a chronically strained health system.

**Questioning:** existential and external regulatory risk; hard to scale on part-time supply; significant external capital needed in a shallow local VC market; seasonality + low frequency → naturally low retention; three hard problems at once (two-sided marketplace + regulation + part-time management).

**Conditions:** written health-law due diligence ("actively legal", not merely "a gap"); first ~20 doctors secured; first ~100 sessions completed with NPS >40 and retention >25%; at least one founder/advisor from the health sector; standard founder vesting; a bridge source ready.

**Overall success probability (self-assessed): ~18–22%** — a *high-risk / high-reward* venture. Recommendation: enter with a small seed, watch the month-6 metrics, then decide on the growth round — don't commit everything on day one.
